The checker accepts unchanged, successful results from the source-aware Hoon
reader as a trusted boundary. It independently rejects malformed graphs and
forks whose supplied cases overlap, but a graph alone cannot reveal source
cases Hoon already collapsed. This fixture compiles a small /sur against zuse,
selects its molds, and passes the actual reader results to inference. It checks
fixed atoms, term singletons including Hoon `%$`, nested faced records,
unnamed unions and opaque standard collections. A nonrecursive `$@` with a
null singleton supports null and record values, including within another
record. Forged nonzero `%n` atoms and overlapping null/term or null/flag cases
stay unsupported. A Hoon boolean's exact two-singleton `%f` fork imports
as `?`, including in either supplied order. Open `%f`, out-of-range values,
duplicates, overlapping zero constants, extra or faced cases and malformed
forks stay unsupported. A structurally unit-like import does not gain unit
construction even when a canonical prelude unit is supplied; constructing
that canonical unit succeeds. It covers matching and
mismatched collections, nested union arguments, and malformed collection names
and arities. An imported `*` can widen a value, while malformed nouns,
overlapping direct or nested union cases, and unknown gate or empty cases
stay unsupported. Distinct tagged cases with a noun payload remain disjoint.
Jibb-written collection molds remain unsupported.

A self-recursive /sur mold is an opaque module/name identity. Distinct names
or modules conflict; aliases, foreign references, unfolding and out-of-scope
names remain unsupported. The fixture checks these limits as well as malformed
references and overlapping recursive graphs. The reader rejects the overlapping
source union instead of publishing a collapsed result. A missing import and a
duplicate supplied mold fail closed. The types output is partial and does not
claim that the module was checked. The same fixture runs under urbit eval and
through -test on the fake ship.

The real-reader mixed-zero case places `%$` and `?` under the same outer tag;
both allow the noun zero, so the reader reports definite overlap. The distinct-tag
`%$`/`?` case and distinct-tag term singletons are successful reader/checker
controls. The reader also reports definite overlap for the collapsed source
whose two cases are both `[%x @ud]`. The overlap error points at the Jibb use site; the Hoon source case
positions are not available in this fixture. These are source-aware
reads and the checker report remains partial.
